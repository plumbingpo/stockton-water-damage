Appliance Leak Water Damage in Stockton: What to Know

Three appliances cause most of the leaks we see. When the supply hose on a washing machine splits, water flows under full pressure until somebody turns it off. A dishwasher tends to leak slowly from a door seal or drain hose, out of sight under the cabinets. The ice maker line on a refrigerator is a thin tube, and it can drip for months behind the unit. No two of these call for the same cleanup.

Our Stockton crew starts by pulling the appliance out and finding where the water went. In kitchens that usually means under the cabinets and beneath the flooring. Laundry room water goes into the wall at the back of the machine and the neighboring rooms, and with an upstairs laundry, the ceiling underneath. We extract what is standing, measure what has soaked in, and set drying equipment sized to the wet area.

Do This First

Your First Steps

  1. Shut the Machine DownIf the outlet and the floor next to it are both dry, end the cycle and take the plug out of the wall. Otherwise, use the breaker to cut its power.
  2. Shut Off the Water LineWashers have hot and cold valves on the wall behind them. Look below the sink for the valve that feeds a dishwasher. Refrigerator lines have a small valve behind or below.
  3. Mop Up What You CanGetting reachable water up with a mop or towels stops it from moving under the cabinets and the floor. Use a vacuum only if it is a wet and dry model.
  4. Leave the Door ShutDo not open a washer or dishwasher that is still full. The entire load of water may spill across the floor if the door is opened mid-cycle.
  5. Document What FailedTake pictures of the hose, valve or fitting that failed and keep the part. Your insurer and the appliance maker may both ask to see it.
The Usual Culprits

What Causes It

  • Refrigerators that leak at floor level because the drain pan has cracked or the defrost drain is clogged.
  • A dishwasher with a failed pump seal, a cracked drain hose or a worn door gasket.
  • A dishwasher that was installed out of level or with a supply connection left loose.
  • Aging rubber supply hoses on a washing machine, which weaken and then split under pressure.
  • Inlet valves that stick open, so the dishwasher or washer keeps taking in water past the fill level.
What Is at Risk

What Happens if You Wait

Kitchen Cabinets

Toe kicks and cabinet sides take on water from underneath when a dishwasher leaks. Swelling and separation follow, and for weeks the soaked cabinet boxes may trap moisture against the wall.

Hidden Mold at the Back

Hardly anyone looks at the dark, warm space behind a dishwasher or refrigerator. Long before it is discovered, a slow drip back there can be growing mold across the floor and wall.

Rooms Next Door

For hours, a failed washer hose can keep releasing water. That water moves beneath walls into bedrooms, closets and hallways, soaking pad and carpet far from the laundry room.

Floors and What Is Under Them

Hardwood, vinyl plank and laminate all let water slip underneath. Boards cup or swell, and the subfloor underneath stays wet long after the surface looks dry.

Insurance

Insurance and Appliance Leak Water Damage

When a machine overflows unexpectedly or a hose bursts, that is typically treated as sudden and accidental, which many homeowners policies cover. The policy typically pays for the water damage, not for the appliance or the failed part. Gradual leaks usually fall outside coverage, so slow seepage at a dishwasher or an ice maker line makes for a tougher claim. How old the hose was and how it was maintained may also come up with some insurers. Hold on to the part that failed, photograph everything ahead of cleanup, and review what your policy says.
