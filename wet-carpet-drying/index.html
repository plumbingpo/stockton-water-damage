Wet Carpet Drying Help in Stockton

There is always more water in a soaked carpet than the surface suggests. Often the pad is full of water and the subfloor is wet beneath it, even when the fibers you touch feel only slightly damp. What you can pull out with towels or a shop vac comes from the top, not from below. In Stockton, we pull the water out, take moisture meter readings on each layer, and place drying equipment that works down to the subfloor.

Two things decide if a carpet is worth saving: the cleanliness of the water and the time it has sat wet. If the water came out of a clean supply line and we reach it fast, the carpet can normally stay down while it dries. With an appliance leak, which is gray water, we often discard the pad, then clean and treat the carpet. After sewage or flood water from outdoors, neither the carpet nor the pad stays.

What It Looks Like

  • A squishy feel under your feet, or wet socks after walking across the room
  • Backing that separates from the carpet when you lift a corner to look
  • Days after the water was cleaned up, the carpet is cool and clammy to the touch
  • Baseboards that have swollen or paint that bubbles low on the wall
  • Wet tack strip showing through as dark, rusty streaks next to the baseboard
  • A wet spot that keeps growing even though no new water has been added

If Nothing Is Done

What Happens if You Wait

The first few hours

Water Moves Outward

Water travels through the pad far beyond the patch you can see. The tack strip starts taking on water as it creeps below the walls toward the room next door.

24 to 48 hours

Smell and Mold Start

That sour wet carpet odor shows up as bacteria multiply in the damp pad. Dust held down in the fibers and the carpet backing both give mold a place to begin.

Days 3 through 7

The Carpet Breaks Down

The latex that holds the backing together breaks down, so the carpet delaminates and ripples. The subfloor swells and the tack strip nails begin to rust.

Past one week

Past Saving

By now the carpet and pad usually have to come out. Often mold has spread to the subfloor and the base of the drywall, which adds to the job.

What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

Water Held in the Pad

Pad can hold many times its weight in water. It keeps feeding moisture into the carpet above and the floor below long after the surface feels dry.

Clean Water Turns Dirty

Even clean water goes bad the longer it sits. As it sits in carpet and pad it picks up soil and bacteria, so a carpet that could have been dried may have to be removed.

Stains From Furniture

Wood stain and metal feet on furniture bleed into wet carpet. Such spots are frequently there for good, while the legs soak up water and swell.

Wet Wall Bottoms

Under the carpet, water travels until it hits the bottom of the walls. It wicks up into the baseboards and drywall unnoticed, since the carpet hides that edge.

Our Process

Our Wet Carpet Drying Process

1

Find the Edges

Thermal imaging and meter readings show us where the water went, closets and the space under walls included. The water category gets identified as well, since it determines what we can keep.

2

Pull Out the Water

We use weighted extraction tools that squeeze water from carpet and pad at once. A wand by itself leaves much more behind, so this step cuts the drying time afterward.

3

Dry All the Layers

The carpet is either pulled back so the pad can go, or left down with air blown beneath it, whichever the job needs. Our air movers and dehumidifiers keep going until meters show a dry carpet and a dry subfloor.

4

Clean and Reinstall

Last, we apply antimicrobial to carpet and subfloor if they need it, replace any pad that was taken out, stretch the carpet back over the tack strip, and give it a cleaning.
