Few emergencies in a building are as hazardous as sewage backing up into it. Anyone who comes in contact with contaminated material is exposed to parasites, bacteria and viruses, among them giardia, E. coli, salmonella and hepatitis, all carried in raw sewage and all dangerous. Across Stockton, Stockton Restoration answers sewage emergencies 24/7, sending trained technicians who arrive with EPA-registered disinfectants and the right protective equipment so your property is decontaminated safely.

The industry classifies sewage as black water, or Category 3, which is the most dangerous and most contaminated type of water damage there is. Because of it, cleanup follows the toughest rules in the trade, which means full protective gear, barriers to contain the area, tearing out any porous material that met sewage and disinfecting all that is left. For Category 3 water damage the reference is IICRC S500, and our crews follow it to bring the building back to safe occupancy.

The cleanup process involves removing all standing sewage, extracting contaminated water from structural materials, removing and properly disposing of every porous material that contacted sewage (carpet, padding, drywall, insulation), thoroughly disinfecting hard surfaces, applying antimicrobial treatments and completing structural drying to prevent secondary mold growth. We also deodorize the affected area with industrial-grade hydroxyl generators and thermal fogging equipment to eliminate lingering sewage odors.

How to Tell You Need Sewage Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Sewage or dark water backing up through floor drains, toilets or tubs
  • Foul-smelling moisture showing up on flooring or along the walls
  • Gurgling sounds from toilets or drains when other fixtures are used
  • Dark residue from sewage left on the basement floor or ringing a drain
  • A rotten egg or sewer smell from the drains or the lowest floor of the home
  • People in the household getting stomach or intestinal symptoms with no clear cause
  • A cleanout that weeps sewage, or wet foul patches in the lawn along the sewer path

What a Professional Brings to Sewage Cleanup

This is one cleanup you should never take on yourself. Because raw sewage is a biological hazard, the work demands trained people, protective equipment (gloves, boots, respirators and Tyvek suits) and disinfection products registered with the EPA and not sold to the public. People exposed to the pathogens in sewage can become seriously ill with skin and respiratory infections, hepatitis or gastrointestinal disease.

Disposal matters as well, since everything the sewage contaminated has to be handled as biohazard waste. It is illegal to throw sewage-contaminated insulation, drywall or carpet in the regular trash, and doing so puts the community and the workers who collect it at risk. A certified restoration company follows proper disposal protocols and works with licensed facilities that accept biohazard waste.

Maintenance Tips for Sewage Cleanup

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Have your sewer line inspected with a camera every 2 to 3 years to catch potential problems
  • Keep cooking oil, grease and food scraps out of the drain, where they build into clogs
  • Toilet paper is the only thing to flush, and that rules out wipes sold as flushable, which still clog
  • Know the location and condition of your sewer cleanout for emergency access
  • Hair and other debris are easy to stop with simple strainers on tub, shower and sink drains
  • Install a backwater valve on your sewer connection to prevent backups from the municipal system

How Our Sewage Cleanup Works

Our systematic approach delivers quality results on every project. Here's how we handle sewage cleanup from start to finish:

1

Hazard Check and Containment

Our team assesses the extent of sewage contamination, identifies safety hazards and establishes containment to prevent cross-contamination of unaffected areas.
