AC Leak Water Damage Help in Stockton

Cooling a house also dries the air, which means the unit is constantly making water it must get rid of. In a healthy system the coil sheds it into a pan, and a narrow drain line carries it away. Once dust and algae block the drain, water backs up until it runs over the edge of the pan. If the air handler sits in a hallway closet or the attic, that water ends up in the nearby floor, wall or ceiling.

People tend to underestimate what a small, steady leak can do. It soaks the stand the unit sits on, climbs into the drywall and spreads beneath the flooring, and by the time you see a mark mold has frequently begun. Our team measures how far it traveled, dries the building materials and fixes the damage. You will still need an HVAC technician to clear the drain or fix the unit, and we can work around their visit.

First Five Minutes

Your First Steps

  1. Turn Off the ACNo more condensate forms once the AC is turned off at the thermostat. Flip the air handler's breaker too when water is close to its wiring.
  2. Keep Clear of Wet ElectricalUntil that area's power has been shut off, keep your hands away from the unit and from any outlet or ceiling light with water in or near it.
  3. Clear Water From the PanIf you can reach the pan safely, soak up or wet-vac the standing water so it stops spilling over. Skip this step if it means crossing a soaked attic.
  4. Dry the Easy SpotsBlot wet carpet, wipe hard floors, set a bucket under any ceiling drip, and move boxes, clothes and furniture away from the closet or the stained area.
  5. Bring In Two ProsAn HVAC technician should clear the drain line, and our team should find out where the water went. Leave the system off until it is fixed.

Why It Happens

Why This Happens

  • Algae, slime and dust from the coil plugging the condensate drain.
  • Poorly connected drain piping, which lets water out before it reaches the outlet.
  • More meltwater than the pan can handle after an iced-over coil thaws.
  • Bare refrigerant lines or ducts that sweat in humid weather and drip on the ceiling.
  • The safety float switch being absent or broken, so the unit kept running.
If It Is Left

Where the Damage Spreads

Ceilings Under the Unit

When the air handler is overhead, the ceiling under it takes the water. The ceiling stains first, then sags, and a section soaked for days can fall into the hallway or bedroom below.

Baseboards and Door Trim

Baseboards and door casings near the closet draw water up from the floor. Fiberboard trim swells and crumbles, so it is usually replaced, not dried.

Insulation Around the Unit

Insulation around an attic unit soaks up overflow and holds it against the ceiling. Wet insulation loses its value and often has to be replaced.

Mold Around the Air Handler

Damp drywall and wood beside the air handler can grow mold within 24 to 48 hours, and the return air can pull spores into the ductwork and through the house.

Will Insurance Pay?

Does Insurance Cover It?

Coverage for an AC leak usually comes down to timing. A sudden pan overflow that you dealt with promptly is the kind of loss homeowners policies often cover. Denial is possible when the adjuster concludes the leak ran for weeks or months, since that reads as a gradual or maintenance problem. Either way, the cost of fixing the AC unit is typically left out. Check your policy, and we can supply readings and photos for the claim.

What Drives the Price

  • The water category, because Category 2 needs more cleaning than Category 1.
  • Square footage of ceiling, wall and flooring that has to be replaced.
  • Length of the leak, counted from the first drip to the day the system was stopped.
  • Whether mold has started and needs containment and removal.
